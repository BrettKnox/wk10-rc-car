WK10-00 · Overview

WK-10, a printable 1/10 on-road pan car

Rear-wheel drive, pivoting rear pod with a locked spool, sliding-pillar front suspension, and an ESP32 that drives it from a phone, a Python script or a second ESP32 used as a handheld transmitter. Every printed part is modelled parametrically in build123d and fits a 220 × 220 mm bed. It is designed and checked in CAD; nothing has been printed or run yet. This packet is for review before building.

Parametric CAD, 25 printed parts Bought parts modelled from maker dimensions Automated fit and kinematic checks Firmware compiles, self-tests pass 3 independent reviews + 2 model passes Not yet printed Not yet run on hardware

Performance numbers come from a first-principles vehicle model (motor Kv and resistance, gearing, rolling resistance, drag, battery C-rate, tire grip). Masses are estimates from the CAD volumes and part listings. Units are millimetres unless stated.

WK10-03 · Layout decisions

Why it is laid out this way

AreaChoiceReason
Class1/10 on-road pan car, 257 mm wheelbase, 164 mm track, 190 mm over the tiresThe owned tires are 1/10 on-road (67 mm, 12 mm hex). 257 mm and 190 mm match the 1/10 GT bodies made for 190 mm pan cars (Venturi GT, Bittydesign Ahura and P-GT3R). The first layout used a 175 mm track, 201 mm over the tires, which no 190 mm body covers. Standard 190 mm bodies fit.
RearPivoting pod, solid 5 mm axle, locked spoolPrinted differentials are the most reported failure in open printable cars (PLA/PETG diffs soften, seize and click). A pod with a spool has no CV joints and one axle. It pushes a little in tight corners.
FrontSliding-pillar kingpin, 3 mm stainless pin, coil spring above the knuckle, printed bump stopUses owned rods and springs; the classic 1/12 pan-car front end. Travel +7 / −5 mm.
SteeringServo horn forward, both tie rods on one bolt 14 mm out; 16 mm knuckle arms angled about 17° outboardSolved with a branch-tracked linkage solver and re-solved for the 164 mm track: 98% Ackermann both ways. An earlier layout that looked right on paper (rearward horn, straight arms) was anti-Ackermann; the check now fails it.
Tie rodsPrinted PETG, two halves joined by a cut M3 stud in heat-set insertsReplaces ball links. Loose 3.6 mm eyes on M3 bolts tolerate the tilt from 7 mm of bump over a 57 mm rod. 5 mm deep bar for buckling margin.
Motor3650 brushless can behind the rear axleAhead of the axle it collided with the pod pivot. Behind, it puts 58% of the mass on the driven wheels, which matters because launch is traction-limited.
PanSplit at X = 86, joined by a block that also carries the servoA 240 mm pan does not fit a 220 mm bed, even diagonally. The joint block does two jobs.
HubsPrinted 12 mm hex hubs with an M3 set bolt on a ground flatThe owned aluminium hubs clamp with an M4 nut and need a threaded axle tip, which plain 5 mm rod does not have.
ControlESP32 as its own Wi-Fi access point (phone joystick, tuning page, HTTP API) plus ESP-NOW from a second ESP32No radio purchase. Every input goes through one failsafe and one arming gate.
BodyPROTOform Venturi GT, 190 mm polycarbonate, posts on the front end and the rear podLexan is about 90 g; a printed shell of the same size would weigh twice that and crack on the first hit. Post heights are computed from the shell, so the posts end 9 mm above the skin. The rear posts move with the pod, so the rear holes are fore-aft slots.
ESP32 mountPrinted cradle and lid, board on end ledges, leads soldered to the pinsThe DevKitC has no mounting holes and its header pins hang 8.5 mm under the board, so the old flat tray could not hold it. The lid pad stops 0.2 mm above the module shield, which captures the board without screws through it. Nothing metal sits near the PCB antenna.

WK10-05 · Structural checks

Closed-form strength checks

Hand-calculation level, not FEA: rectangular-section beam theory with material allowables that already include a knockdown for printed parts (PLA 40 MPa, PETG 40 MPa, steel 250 MPa). Round sections are converted to the square with the same second moment. Load cases are deliberately harsh (about 10 g on one corner, which is a kerb strike). Fatigue, layer-adhesion direction and creep are not modelled; those are the places your judgement is most useful.

The bar is now 5 mm deep away from the eyes (it still prints flat), the rod is shorter after the track change, and the CAD build fails if the buckling factor drops under 2.
WK10-06 · Fits and tolerances

Fits and tolerances

FDM holes print about 0.2 mm under size on this printer class, so every bore is modelled 0.2 mm over its target. That allowance (HOLE_COMP) is assumed until the calibration coupon is printed and measured; it is one parameter, and the whole set regenerates from it. Untoleranced printed dimensions: ±0.2 mm.

Safety model

MechanismBehaviour
Single writerA 100 Hz control task (priority 10, core 1) is the only code that sets the servo and ESC pulses. The web server and OTA, which can block for seconds, only hand it commands under a spinlock.
FailsafeNo valid command for 250 ms (configurable 100 to 500 ms) gives neutral throttle and centre steering. Pulses keep running so the ESC sees neutral, not a frozen value.
Arming gateThrottle passes only after the dead-man has been seen released on the current source since boot, since the last failsafe and since the last source change. A brownout reboot with the trigger held does not launch the car.
WatchdogIf the control task stalls for 300 ms, the watchdog interrupt detaches GPIO19 and holds it low before the chip resets, so the ESC drops to its own signal-loss failsafe.
LimitsForward power capped at 50% until raised; reverse capped at half; throttle slew 4 full-scale/s; low-pack limp mode at 3.4 V per cell (with the divider).
ESC calibrationA 30 s phone-only mode sends true 1000 to 2000 µs end points; it ends if the phone loses control. Without it the ESC would learn the 50% cap as full power.
InputsEvery setting is range-checked on the car; transmitter packets need the bound MAC, a checksum and a rising sequence number; settings and OTA are refused unless the car is parked.
VerificationBoth sketches compile (car 77% of flash). The control maths self-test runs at boot and on the PC; three deliberately broken copies of it fail. The Python client is tested against a fake car.

Review log

The design was reviewed three times by an independent agent that read the code and model without the author's notes, again while this packet was assembled, and a fourth time when every bought part was replaced with a model built from its maker's dimensions. The detailed parts found what the plain boxes had hidden. Every finding below was fixed and is covered by a check where one is possible.

WK10-13 · Questions for you

Where a mechanical engineer's eye helps most

#Question
1Knuckles print with the axle bore vertical, so wheel loads bend the housing across layer lines. Is 100% infill PETG enough there, or should the bearing housing get a different orientation or a metal sleeve?
2The motor plate is PETG at up to about 60 °C with an M3-clamped motor. How worried should I be about creep loosening the gear mesh over a few runs?
3The sliding pillar runs a PETG knuckle (3.5 mm bore) on a 3 mm stainless pin with grease. Stiction and wear: acceptable, or bush it with brass tube?
4The kingpin and rear axle sit at a safety factor of about 2.4 for a 10 g corner strike. Is that load case realistic for a 1.2 kg car hitting a kerb at 50 km/h?
5The pan is split at X = 86 and spliced by an 8 mm block with 8 M3 screws. Does the joint need more length, or will the side rails carry the bending?
6The rear pod has one coil spring and no damper. Is a printed friction damper or an oil damper worth adding before the first run?
7Anything on the drawings you would dimension, tolerance or datum differently for a part that comes off an FDM printer?

The CAD source, STEP files, print-ready STLs and these drawings come as a separate zip from Brett. The whole model regenerates from one Python file, so any change you suggest can be re-checked in about a minute.
